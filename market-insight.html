Structured Product Read-Through
▾

Wednesday's quarter-point hike to 3.75-4.00% pushed the 2-year to 4.74% and the 10-year to 5.01%, a 19-year high, lifting coupon levels on new autocallable and yield-enhancement issuance. The curve has flattened further to 27bp from 33bp a week ago, trimming the pickup available from extending duration on capital-protected notes. Credit is still well-behaved — IG and HY OAS both tightened 2-6bp even as rates sold off — so we'd lean into rate-driven coupon pickup over credit risk for now.

Regional Outlook — Asia / Hong Kong
▾

Hong Kong equities bounced 0.60% to 24,751 today, clawing back a sliver of a week that saw the Hang Seng shed roughly 3.3% over five sessions on an AI-linked tech rout and the Fed's hike. The CSI 300 outperformed, up 1.06% to 4,507, as domestic support held firmer than offshore sentiment. Southbound flows stayed net positive through the rout, cushioning rather than reversing the slide. USD/HKD sits at 7.8293, comfortably inside the peg, with no HIBOR stress evident. Today's bounce reads as stabilisation, not an all-clear — Fed-driven yield volatility remains the swing factor into month-end.

Top Client Question of the Day
▾

"The Fed just delivered its first hike since 2023 and signalled more — should we be adding duration now, or wait for yields to climb further?"

House view

Add it now, in tranches. The 10-year at 5.01% is already a 19-year high, and much of the front-end hiking path is priced in — waiting risks missing this level if long yields stall while the Fed keeps lifting short rates. Ladder into 5-7 year duration; the flat 27bp 2s10s argues against stretching further out the curve for marginal pickup. Pair with credit, where IG and HY spreads actually tightened through the rate shock.
