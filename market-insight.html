Structured Product Read-Through
▾

The Iran-driven oil spike pushed both ends of the curve higher, with 10Y touching its highest since January 2025 at 4.75% while 2s10s only marginally re-steepened to 39bp — a backdrop that lifts the funding leg on new autocallable coupons without much help from duration extension. MOVE jumped 6.1% to 75.32, the sharpest bond-vol move in weeks, even as VIX held near cycle lows at 14.43 — that divergence raises hedging costs on capital-protected notes with embedded duration while leaving equity-linked coupon funding largely unmoved. Treat this as a geopolitical repricing rather than a durable vol regime shift.

Regional Outlook — Asia / Hong Kong
▾

Hong Kong opened the week under pressure from the Iran-driven oil spike but closed little changed: Hang Seng eased 0.07% to 25,566.99 while HSCEI added 0.27% to 8,513.18 and CSI 300 rose 0.35% to 4,625.09, helped by China's August manufacturing PMI improving to 49.8 from July's 49.2 — still contractionary but the best print since spring. USD/HKD held 7.8391, comfortably inside the peg band. Higher US front-end yields keep HIBOR-USD carry attractive for property borrowers watching refinancing costs, while the oil spike is a fresh headwind for HK's import bill and a mild tailwind for energy-linked mandates.

Top Client Question of the Day
▾

"Oil just spiked and the 10-year hit its highest yield since January 2025 on the Iran flare-up — do we chase yields higher, or is this a geopolitical air-pocket we should look through?"

House view

Look through it, but use the move. Middle East risk premia in oil typically fade once the immediate escalation cools, and a 10Y print at cycle highs on a supply-driven, not demand-driven, oil shock is a buying opportunity for income-seeking mandates rather than a reason to chase duration further out. We would add selectively into 7-10Y allocations on this back-up rather than wait for a pullback that a single de-escalation headline could erase overnight.
